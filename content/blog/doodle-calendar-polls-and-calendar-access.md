No. You can answer a poll or fill in an availability grid without connecting a calendar. A Doodle calendar connection, like the one in any scheduling tool that offers it, is a convenience. It can show your existing events beside the poll and put the chosen meeting on your calendar. Whether that is worth granting access depends on how many polls you answer and how much of your week is on that calendar.

## Two ways a tool learns when you are free

**You tell it.** You look at your own calendar and mark the times you can make. The tool knows only what you marked.

**It reads your calendar.** You grant access once, and the tool can see when you have events.

Both end with a list of times you can make. They differ in effort, in accuracy and in what you share.

## The trade

|                                     | You mark your free time             | The tool reads your calendar              |
| ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Setup                               | None                                | Grant access once per calendar            |
| Effort per poll                     | Check your calendar, then mark      | Lower. Conflicts can be shown to you.     |
| Covers plans not on the calendar    | Yes. You know about the school run. | No. An empty hour looks free.             |
| Lets you decline a "free" hour      | Yes. You mark only what you want.   | Only if you can override it.              |
| What the tool can see               | The slots you marked                | Whatever the permission you grant allows  |
| Works for people in other companies | Yes                                 | Each person decides for their own account |
| Getting the event on the calendar   | You add it, often from a link       | Often done for you                        |

## Questions to ask before you connect

- What does the permission screen list? Read access to event details is more than read access to busy times.
- Does your employer allow third-party apps on the work calendar?
- Can you disconnect later, and where?
- Is the connection part of the free plan? Plans change, so read the tool's current pricing page.

## When to skip calendar access

Skip it when you answer a few polls a year, when your calendar does not reflect your real availability, or when the group spans organizations and half of them will not connect anyway. Marking free time by hand is a small job, and it is the only way to say "that hour is empty and I still do not want a meeting in it".

## When it earns its place

Connect when you schedule many meetings, your calendar is accurate, and the tool both shows your conflicts and creates the event. The time saved adds up.
