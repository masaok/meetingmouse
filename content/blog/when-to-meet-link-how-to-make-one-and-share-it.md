A when to meet link is a single URL that opens an availability grid. You make the grid once, send the link to the group, and each person marks the times they are free. The link replaces a thread of proposals with one page that counts the overlap for you.

## What sits behind the link

The page holds three things. The dates and hours you are asking about, a way for each person to mark their free time, and a combined view that shows where the group overlaps. The method behind that combined view is in [when to meet: find a time that works for everyone](/blog/when-to-meet-find-a-time-that-works-for-everyone).

## Make the link

1. **Pick a tool.** Any availability grid tool gives you a link. If you have not chosen one, [what to look for in a when to meet app](/blog/when-to-meet-app-what-to-look-for) lists the criteria.
2. **Name the meeting.** Use the name people will recognize, such as "Q4 planning", not "Meeting".
3. **Choose the dates.** Offer one or two weeks. More than that and people stop filling in the grid.
4. **Set the hours.** Offer only hours when the meeting could happen.
5. **Copy the link** from the page the tool gives you.

## Share it so people answer

A bare link gets ignored. Put four things beside it.

- What the meeting is and how long it will run.
- The link.
- A deadline for answering.
- What happens after the deadline.

Here is a message you can copy.

```text
Planning session for the Q4 roadmap, 60 minutes.
Mark every time you are free here: <link>
Please answer by Thursday at noon.
On Thursday afternoon I will pick the slot most of us can make and send the invite.
```

Post it where the group already talks. Send one reminder a day before the deadline, and name the people who have not answered.

## Common mistakes

- **Sending the link with no deadline.** People mean to answer later and do not.
- **Offering too many days.** A long grid looks like work.
- **Forgetting time zones.** Say which zone the grid uses, or pick a tool that converts for each viewer.
- **Never closing the loop.** Announce the chosen time in the same place you shared the link.

## When the group is in Slack, you can skip the link

A link asks people to leave the conversation, open a page and come back. If everyone is in one Slack channel, the poll can be a message in that channel.

Meeting Mouse works this way. Running `/meet` with a title posts the poll as one message, and each person clicks Add my availability to answer in a form in Slack. The form can also link to a grid page for dragging across the slots. That grid link is personal. It is signed for one person and one poll, it works for 30 days, and whoever holds it can change that person's answer, so do not forward it. Nobody has to paste a shared URL, and the channel message shows the result. [How Meeting Mouse works](/#how) walks through it.
