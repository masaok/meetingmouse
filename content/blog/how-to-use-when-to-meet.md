Here is how to use when to meet tools, the free availability grids of which When2meet is the best known. You create an event with a set of dates and a range of hours, share its link, and each person paints the times they are free. The page then shades a second grid by how many people can make each slot. The steps below follow When2meet's flow, and they apply to any tool built on the same idea.

## The six steps

1. **Create the event.** Give it a name people will recognize.
2. **Choose the dates and the hours.** Pick the days you are considering, then the earliest and latest time a meeting could start and end. Check the time zone setting before you continue.
3. **Share the link.** Creating the event gives you a URL. Send it to the group with a deadline. [How to make and share a when to meet link](/blog/when-to-meet-link-how-to-make-one-and-share-it) has a message you can copy.
4. **Sign in with a name.** Each person types a name to identify their answer. No account is needed.
5. **Paint your free time.** Click and drag across the grid to mark the slots you can make. Drag again over marked slots to clear them.
6. **Read the group grid.** A second grid shows everyone's answers together, darker where more people are free. Pick the darkest slot that is long enough for the meeting.

The site can change, so treat the labels above as a guide to the flow and not as exact button names.

## Tips that save a round of messages

- **Keep the range short.** One or two weeks of dates and only working hours.
- **Say what "free" means.** Tell people whether to mark times they prefer or every time they could manage.
- **Use the same name each time.** If you come back to edit, sign in with the name you used before so you update your answer and do not add a second one.
- **Mind the time zone.** If your group spans zones, ask each person to confirm the zone shown on the page before painting.
- **Set a deadline and close the loop.** The grid does not announce a result. Someone has to read it, choose and tell the group.

## What the grid does not do

An availability grid of this kind stops at the picture. It does not pick the time, tell the group, or create the calendar event. Plan to do those three things yourself once the answers are in.

It also asks everyone to leave the conversation, open a page and remember to come back. For a group spread over several organizations that is a fair price. For a team that already shares a chat channel, it is a step you can remove.

## The same flow inside Slack

Meeting Mouse follows the same flow with two steps removed. Nobody shares a link and nobody types a name, because the poll is a channel message and people answer as their Slack user. Run `/when` with a title in a channel and choose the dates, the daily window and the slot length. Each person clicks Add my availability and clicks the times that work in a form, in their own time zone, and each click saves. The form links to a page for dragging across a grid, with the group's availability beside your own. The message updates with the best times, the organizer picks one, and a thread reply carries an Add to Google Calendar link. [See how Meeting Mouse works](/#how).
