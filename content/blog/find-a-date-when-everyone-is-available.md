To find a date when everyone is available, list every candidate date, ask each person to mark all the dates they can make, and count. The date with a full count wins. If no date has a full count, a rule you set in advance decides. This works for an offsite, a dinner, a workshop or any event where the day matters more than the hour.

## Step 1. List the candidate dates

Write down every date that could work for the event itself. Remove the ones ruled out by the venue, by holidays or by a key person you already know is away. Six to ten candidates is plenty.

## Step 2. Ask for every date, not a favorite

Ask each person to mark all the dates they can make. If people name only their favorite, you learn one date per person and cannot see the overlap.

Give a deadline. [How to ask a group when we can meet](/blog/how-to-ask-when-we-can-meet) has wording you can copy.

## Step 3. Count

Put the answers in a table, one row per date.

| Date        | Ana | Ben | Chi | Dev | Eli | Can make it |
| ----------- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | ----------- |
| Tue, Nov 3  | yes | yes |     | yes | yes | 4           |
| Thu, Nov 5  | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes | 5           |
| Tue, Nov 10 | yes |     | yes | yes |     | 3           |
| Wed, Nov 11 |     | yes | yes | yes | yes | 4           |

Thursday, November 5 has all five, so that is the date.

## Step 4. Decide what to do when no date has everyone

With more than a handful of people, a full count is rare. Decide the rule before you look at the answers, so the choice is not personal.

- **Name the people who must attend.** Drop every date that one of them cannot make.
- **Take the highest count** among the dates that remain.
- **Break a tie** with the earlier date, or with the date the must-attend people prefer.
- **Tell the people who cannot come** before you announce, and say what they will get instead.

## Step 5. Lock the date, then find the hour

Announce the date in the place you asked, and put a hold on calendars the same day. The longer the gap between the poll and the invite, the more answers go stale.

If the hour is also open, run a second round for that day only. That round is a question of times, and the method is in [when to meet: find a time that works for everyone](/blog/when-to-meet-find-a-time-that-works-for-everyone).

Meeting Mouse is built for the second round and can cover both. A poll offers up to 14 dates with a daily window of hours, so people mark the hours they are free on each candidate date. The channel message then shows who is free when and ranks the best times by headcount. A date whose slots show everyone free is a date when the group is available.
