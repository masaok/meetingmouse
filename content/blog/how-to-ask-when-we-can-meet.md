The fastest way to settle when we can meet is to ask one clear question with a short date range and a deadline, and to collect the answers in one place. An open question such as "when is good for everyone?" gets slow, partial replies. A bounded question gets answers you can count.

## What a good ask contains

| Part                  | Why it matters                                           | Example                         |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| The meeting           | People decide how much to move for it                    | "Kickoff for the billing work"  |
| The length            | A 30-minute gap is no use for a 90-minute meeting        | "60 minutes"                    |
| The date range        | An open range invites "any time", which is not an answer | "Mon 12 to Fri 16"              |
| Where to answer       | Replies scattered over chat and email cannot be counted  | One poll or one thread          |
| The deadline          | Without one, the last reply never comes                  | "By Wednesday at noon"          |
| What you will do next | People answer when they know the question will be closed | "I will send the invite Friday" |

## Templates you can copy

For two or three people, a direct question is enough.

```text
Can you do 30 minutes on Tuesday or Wednesday afternoon?
Tell me every hour that works and I will pick one today.
```

For a group in chat, point at one place to answer.

```text
We need 60 minutes for the billing kickoff next week.
Mark every time you are free in the poll below by Wednesday at noon.
I will pick the time most of us can make and send the invite on Wednesday afternoon.
```

For email, where people may be in other companies.

```text
Subject: Finding a time for the billing kickoff (60 min, week of the 12th)

Hello all,

Please mark every time you could join at this link: <link>
The grid shows times in your own time zone.
I will close it on Wednesday at noon and send an invite for the slot most of us can make.
```

For a reminder.

```text
Three of us have answered the kickoff poll. Sam and Priya, could you add your times by noon tomorrow?
```

## Ask for free time, not for a vote

"Does Tuesday at 2 work?" gets a yes or a no, and a no sends you back to the start. "Mark every time you are free" gets you the whole picture in one round. [When to meet: find a time that works for everyone](/blog/when-to-meet-find-a-time-that-works-for-everyone) explains why counting overlap beats trading proposals.

## Close the question

Pick the slot with the most people. If someone cannot make it, tell them directly and offer notes or a recording. Announce the time in the place you asked, then send the calendar invite.

## In Slack, the ask and the answer can be one message

If the group shares a Slack channel, Meeting Mouse turns the ask into a poll. `/when Billing kickoff` posts one message with an Add my availability button, each person marks their free slots in their own time zone, and the message lists the best times by headcount. The organizer then picks the final time, and a thread reply carries an Add to Google Calendar link. See [how Meeting Mouse works](/#how).

If you need to send a link to people outside Slack, read [how to make and share a when to meet link](/blog/when-to-meet-link-how-to-make-one-and-share-it).
